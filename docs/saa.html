Background from the South Atlantic Anomaly

21 days of continuous GBM data, 220817 to 220906 · 189 SAA passages · built 2026-11-09

Fermi passes through the South Atlantic Anomaly several times a day. The detectors are switched off while it does, and when they come back on they are counting their own housing: charged particles have activated the spacecraft, and that activation decays away over the following minutes. It is a real background component, and it is one of the things a physical background model has to get right.

None of this is visible anywhere else in this project. Every other measurement here is trigger-centred, covering a few hundred seconds around a burst, and the activation decays over a span many times longer than that. Inside such a window it is an almost constant offset, absorbed whole by the background polynomial's constant term. Seeing it at all needs continuous days.

Is the activation harder than the background?

Markedly so. Splitting the same fold by CTIME's eight energy channels, the excess in the first minute climbs from about a tenth in the softest channel to roughly three quarters around half an MeV — where the de-excitation lines of activated material sit. The two softest channels go below their quiet level after a few minutes, which is orbital rather than activation: an SAA exit happens at a particular point in the orbit, and the soft background tracks orbital position.

What this is not

A single exponential is a convenience rather than the truth. The profile dips about 4% below the 60–90 minute reference around 30–45 minutes, so the baseline is not flat — almost certainly orbital, since geomagnetic latitude varies on the same roughly 96 minute period and is itself a background driver. The fit also only sees the fast component; a slower one takes hours and needs a whole day modelled at once.

These numbers are a direct measurement, not a model. Nothing here is fitted jointly with the other background components — cosmic diffuse, Earth albedo, point sources — which is what a physical background model such as Biltzinger et al. (2020) does, and which is the obvious next step.
