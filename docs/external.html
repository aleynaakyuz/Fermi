GBM bursts that somebody else pinned down

Of the 4,400 GRBs in the Fermi GBM burst catalog, 969 carry a position that GBM could not have produced on its own. GBM's ground localization bottoms out at a systematic floor of 2–4 degrees, so every burst here was located by another instrument — usually Swift, sometimes the Fermi LAT, occasionally something that only shows up in a circular.

How these were identified

Flagged by error radius: the GBM catalog adopts an external position when one exists, and GBM alone cannot reach better than a couple of degrees. Anything listed below 0.5° is carrying somebody else's answer. Attributed by cross-match: the Fermi LAT catalog joins directly on its GBM designation; Swift is matched on trigger time within 60 s.

The two signals agree well. Swift matches sit a median 0.0056° (20 arcsec) from the position the GBM catalog lists, with 92% inside 0.1° — which is what it looks like when the catalog has simply adopted the Swift position outright.

LAT is different: only 36% of LAT matches land within 0.1°, because the GBM catalog generally does not adopt LAT positions. Those rows are genuine joint detections where the listed position is still GBM's own. Worth keeping in mind if you use this as a truth set — a LAT match does not guarantee a precise catalog position.

Six matches are flagged suspect: the catalog carries a precise adopted position, but the burst found by cross-match sits more than a degree away, which means the match caught the wrong burst rather than that the position is wrong. bn160625945 is the clearest case — GRB 160625B paired with Swift's separate GRB 160625A. A further 64 rows show large offsets for an innocent reason: the catalog holds GBM's own position there, so the offset is simply GBM's localization error.

The 158 unattributed rows are all error-radius flagged, so they do have external positions, from instruments not queried here or from afterglows reported only in GCN circulars. They cluster heavily in recent years, where the cross-match catalogs have not caught up yet.
